A Romantic Weekend on Lake Orta

No cars in the village, an island whose monastery asks you to stop talking, and a lake small enough to see both shores at once. A two-night itinerary that does not try to do too much.

The French travel press has settled on a description of Lake Orta — le lac le plus romantique du Piémont, the most romantic lake in Piedmont — and for once the marketing is doing something honest. The reason is structural rather than sentimental: the village centre has no cars in it, the lake is narrow enough to feel enclosed, and the island in the middle is occupied by a monastery that asks visitors to be quiet. Put together, that produces a weekend that is genuinely different from one on a bigger lake.

Here is how we would spend two nights.

Friday evening: arrive and do nothing

Get in before dark if you can. Leave the car — the village is pedestrian and you will not need it again — and walk down into Orta San Giulio as the light goes. Piazza Motta opens straight onto the water, facing the island, and the whole square turns amber for about twenty minutes before dusk.

Eat late and simply the first night. The point of Friday is to stop travelling.

Saturday morning: the island

The boat across to Isola San Giulio takes a few minutes. The island has one circular lane, and the Benedictine monastery has posted signs along it that read, in four languages, "the island of silence welcomes you". Walking it clockwise, the signs are captioned the way of silence; walking back the other way, the way of meditation. It sounds like a gimmick and it is not — people genuinely stop talking, and the effect on a couple who have been in a car for six hours is remarkable.

Go early. By eleven the day-trippers arrive and the silence becomes theoretical.

Saturday afternoon: climb something

Two options, both about twenty minutes uphill. The Sacro Monte, a UNESCO site of twenty frescoed chapels in a wood directly above the village, with viewpoints along the path where the whole lake opens beneath you. Or, across the water, the terrace at Madonna del Sasso, which gives the single widest view of the lake there is.

Either way you end up somewhere high and quiet in the late afternoon, which is the point.

Saturday night: the dinner

If you are going to spend money on one thing, spend it here. Villa Crespi — Antonino Cannavacciuolo's three-Michelin-star restaurant, in a Moorish-revival villa ten minutes from the village — is a destination in its own right, and people fly in for it. Book well ahead; this is not a walk-in.

If that is not the evening you want, Orta has small places on and just off the piazza where you can eat well for a tenth of the price, at a table facing the island. Neither choice is wrong.

Sunday: the slow version

Take a boat out onto the lake with no destination, or walk the shore path north from the village. Have a long lunch. Leave later than you planned.

Where to stay, and why it matters here

On a romantic weekend the accommodation is not incidental — it is most of the experience. A hotel room on a busy shore road is not the same thing as a house with its own garden running down to the water.

Villa Volpe sits on the lakefront just outside the pedestrian zone, with a private beach three metres from the door and the island in view. You can swim before breakfast without meeting anyone, and walk into the village in a few minutes when you want to. For two people it is more space than you need, which is rather the point.

One honest caveat

July and August are the exception to everything above. The village is busy, the boats are full, and the silence on the island has to be looked for. If the romantic weekend is the point rather than the swimming, come in May or in October. The lake is the same and the crowd is gone.
